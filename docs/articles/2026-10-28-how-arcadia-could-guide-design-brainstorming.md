# How Arcadia could guide design brainstorming

On 2026-10-28 the operator raised an idea: Arcadia should help with
brainstorming a design by using the established practices of the design field,
whether that is a game, a website, an app or a song, and the list of fields
should have no fixed end. This note describes the proposal that came out of
that idea. It is a proposal only. No design facility exists in Arcadia today,
and nothing below should be read as a feature you can use.

## What the rehearsals found, in short

The proposal has been rehearsed on paper twice, with simulated operators and no
software. These are agents playing people, so the results are evidence from a
rehearsal, not proof about real operators.

- **The field-neutral shape held.** In the second round, five simulated operators
  across three fields (two websites, two games, and one song, the first time
  this design was tried on music) went through the same core with no field
  guide. None needed a field-specific change.
- **The record was the weak point.** Only one of the five accepted the written
  record unchanged on the first pass. Most recurring faults were in how the
  facilitator records and speaks: the record said more or less than the
  operator said; core jargon leaked into conversation; and next moves lacked an
  owner, a "when" and an honest scope. Two faults exposed gaps in the core
  itself: it assumed a single decision owner, and parked ideas had no way back.
- **Revision 5 is a draft that proposes changes for those faults.** It adds Owner
  and revival conditions for the two core gaps, plus a few other concepts and
  three requirements, described below. It is a draft awaiting operator review. It
  has not been submitted as an Agent Ask.

## The problem

In this author's framing, brainstorming works best when it is loose and
Arcadia's governed work works best when it is strict. A vague idea for a game does not fit an Action with
acceptance criteria, yet forcing it into one too early either loses the idea or
bends it into the wrong shape.

Today you have a few partial homes. The Back Burner keeps an idea on a shelf
without making it work. An Agent Ask, Arcadia's structured request for a
change, can record a proposal. Planning runs in two phases: Phase 1 is an
interview that agrees an Outcome, and Phase 2 turns that Outcome into a Plan.
(The Ask vocabulary is explained in
`2026-10-23-how-an-idea-becomes-governed-work.md`.) What seems to be missing,
in this author's reading, is the middle: a way to keep many half-formed ideas,
see how they affect each other, and know what to do about each one next.

## The proposed core (revision 5 draft)

The proposal avoids building a game tool, then a website tool, then a song
tool. It defines one small core that makes no assumption about the field. It
is written in plain language, not as a schema. The "owner" below means a
design's decision owner, not an Arcadia operator.

- **Design brief.** One checked-in document per design. Its top holds the
  experience target, the principles and the stated scope, each attributed to
  whoever said it.
- **Experience target.** The moment or result the whole design is judged
  against, in the owners' words. If none has been stated, one question asks for
  it. A design may have more than one.
- **Principles.** The owners' convictions and taste, recorded at the strength
  they were given: a rule ("no passwords, ever") or a feel. Only the owner
  rewords a principle.
- **Learned (new in revision 5).** What a test or past attempt showed, scoped to
  exactly what was tried. It is evidence, not a principle, and becomes a
  principle only when the owner says so.
- **Ideas.** Verbatim captures of any size or medium, kept before any question
  is asked. A photo, a voice memo or a sketch is kept as the original, with the
  owner's own description beside it, never transcribed or reworded. An idea can
  be marked "thinking out loud (new in revision 5)": kept, with no position.
- **Elements.** The parts of the design that matter. They inform each other, and
  cycles are allowed. An idea can stay attached to several elements as one
  tangle; separating them is only ever a suggestion. A link the facilitator
  infers is shown as a suggestion until the owner confirms it. An element may
  be recorded as one route to the experience target, which keeps cheaper
  substitutes open.
- **Owner (new in revision 5).** Who decides each element: one person,
  everyone jointly, or one person after a condition. The default is the single
  operator. An absent owner's views stay attributed to them and are never
  merged into the present owner's.
- **Positions.** Where an item stands: open, candidates, leaning, chosen,
  evidenced by a probe, or rejected. Rejected stays on record with no next
  move, and the owner may still act against it. Options are recorded exactly as
  wide as they were stated.
- **Next moves.** Each open item gets one. It names who does it, when in the
  owner's terms (a date, or a step such as "before I write a second verse"), and
  lists only the items it actually settles. The kinds are: ask a question,
  decide (with each option carrying its cost in the owner's numbers), probe
  (the cheapest experiment that produces something to look at), wait on another
  named move, or park. Advice, such as a single warning, is said once in
  conversation and is never an item.
- **Parking, with a way back (changed in revision 5).** Every park carries a
  revival condition, near or far: revisit at the end of this design, a date, a
  trigger, or kept out of view until a date or step. Ideas outside this design
  go to the Back Burner with a revival condition. An urgent real-world task
  outside the design would go to the operator's to-do, not the shelf (proposed,
  pending Decision 4).
- **Visible (new in revision 5).** Items the owner marks as energizing stay in
  view whatever their position or next move.
- **Field guides (optional).** Data for a given field: its typical elements,
  competing frameworks, questions and probe types. Every entry would carry
  provenance: a cited source, operator practice, or agent inference. Without a
  guide the core still works, and elements emerge from the ideas.
- **Ready to commit.** The core elements are chosen, the riskiest assumption
  behind the experience target has been probed or knowingly accepted, and
  every other open item has a next move with an owner. Not ready is the normal
  end of a first session. The useful output then is the probes and the open
  Decisions.

## How the facilitator's discipline changes

Round 2 pointed mostly at the facilitator's habits, and at two gaps in the core.
Revision 5 proposes changes for each. Three are new requirements.

**Record fidelity (REQ-010).** The record states exactly what was said, no more
and no less. In the round 2 logs this slipped in several directions:

- Strength: a feel written down as a rule. The song persona wanted "chord barely
  changes" kept as a feel, not a law.
- Scope: one playtest result promoted into a principle. The game persona asked
  for "a thing we learned, not a rule". That is why Learned exists.
- Width: an option narrowed beyond what was said. The facilitator wrote "sell
  without a button" where the persona had only not yet seen a buy button that
  felt right.
- Direction: a link inferred the wrong way round, so inferred links now show as
  suggestions.

Under the new requirement, an owner who says "that isn't what I said" about any
row means the record has failed.

**Owners (REQ-011).** The co-founder game persona showed that the core assumed a
single decision-maker. His answer was "money, Ana; loop, both; art, Dev *after*
the two-day pass". Each element now has a decision owner, absent people's views
stay attributed, and a joint decision counts as chosen only when every joint
owner has chosen.

**Revival (REQ-012).** Every park carries a condition for coming back, including
the short "end of this design" list, which one persona called where ideas go to
die. A date, a trigger, or "don't let it come back up before" are all allowed.

Two older rules were tightened rather than added. First, the core's own words
("probe", "waits on", "position") stay internal. Two personas reacted to the
word "probe" ("it makes it sound like somebody's going to run a test on me"),
and a third found the three-part question about who, how and how long to be
like a clipboard. The facilitator says "the test" or "not decided", and asks
conversationally. Second, a next move without an owner is no longer a next move:
a persona objected to "ask, later: ask who?" and to a move that carried his name
for something he was not doing. Facilitator-owned moves are marked as the
facilitator's.

Smaller changes in the same draft: a probe's own pass mark can become a
principle when the owner states it as one ("the real version has to be about as
easy as a text"), and an owner's test criterion stays the owner's.

## How ideas reach governed work

A ready design would produce previews of Agent Asks and nothing more: the brief
as an `artifact` Ask, plus Outcome, Milestone and Decision Asks, and the list
of probes for Phase 2 planning. Nothing approves itself, and the brief grants
no authority. Existing rules stay in force. Work that is already settled
changes only through the existing amendment path. Because Arcadia's governance
has a single human operator, revision 5 leaves open how other owners, such as a
co-founder or a bandmate, are named in a brief; only the operator settles Asks.

## The five round 2 walk-throughs

Agents running on the Fable model played five operators while the facilitator
(Ellis, the planner) followed the revision 4 core by hand, with no field guide.
None of the five designs was ready to commit, which the core treats as the
normal end of a first session.

| Persona | Field | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rosa | Website: community fridges | Accepted the rest of the list as written. One urgent item was wrongly shelved. She settled a conflict herself by rewording her own principle to "no passwords, ever". |
| Teo | Website: studio ceramicist | Would not accept it as written: "two of the rows say things about me that aren't what I said". Accepted with fixes. |
| June | Game: physical two-player train card game | Accepted after edits, ending with "Record accepted as written". |
| Dev | Game: online party game, absent co-founder Ana | Accepted after four edits. |
| Sam | Song: wedding song due in 16 days | Accepted after three edits. No song-specific change to the core was needed. |

What held, according to the round 2 synthesis: verbatim capture of a photo and an audio
memo, the "leaning" position, saying advice once, letting the operator set how a
test is judged and how long it takes, and reviving a parked idea by date. Each
of these is a note from a simulated run.

## What the first round and the reviews changed

Per the coordinator (Rowan), Ellis (Engineer, working as planner) drafted the
proposal and an independent plan-critic, a role that challenges plans,
critiqued it. An adversarial review judged revision 2 "does not generalize as
written" (as relayed), and the changes after it produced revision 3. Those review reports
are relayed, not checked in. A first round of two walk-throughs (one website
persona, one game persona) then "passed with changes", leading to revision 4:

- Exactly one next move was too strict, so "waits on" became a valid move.
- Parking needed a near "revisit at the end of this design" list, separate from the Back Burner.
- "Leaning" became a real position, so a name could be "Gap Day (leaning)".
- A warning is said once in conversation and is never an item.
- "Experience target" became an explicit part of the core, after the game persona's twentieth-night payoff was first squeezed into a "progression" element.
- Tangles of ideas are allowed, and the guide stops asking once declined.
- "Rejected" became a position kept on record, after the facilitator parked a rejected idea as "nearby" and the persona corrected it to "no".
- Probe size belongs to the operator, not the facilitator ("ten minutes is a lie, it'll be a weekend").
- Principles, such as that the design should sometimes get worse for no reason, got a place in the brief.

## What is not done

- **Nothing is built.** There is no Plan, Action, Decision or code. Revision 4
  was submitted as Agent Ask request
  `proposal-field-neutral-design-guidance-2026-10-28` and accepted on
  2026-10-28 as preserved evidence only. Nothing about the plan or queue changed.
- **Revision 5 is a draft.** It exists as a draft awaiting operator review. It
  has not been submitted as an Agent Ask. A low-confidence inference lesson from
  round 2 was captured in the workspace (a signal, not authority).
- **No real use.** All seven walk-throughs used simulated operators. The first
  milestone is still a paper walk-through with the operator's real website and
  game ideas. A website field guide follows, then a game field guide.
- **Revision 5 itself is untested.** Its new rules came from round 2 findings;
  no run has used them yet.
- **Five Decisions are open and none has been opened or answered:**
  the architecture (core only, core plus guides as data, built-in fields or a
  plugin system); a design-facilitation role; who writes field guides; how
  probes and urgent to-dos the operator performs are recorded; and the
  condition for reviving the proposal.
- **Open Phase 2 questions include:** where a brief lives before a Project exists, the
  field-guide format and home, the surface for viewing a brief, and how owners
  who are not Arcadia operators are named.
- **Not for publication yet.** No public pull request pins these claims, so
  this draft is not eligible for Field Notes under Decision 0120.
