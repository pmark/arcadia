# Live demo run-sheet — 2026-08-21

**Audience:** one technical peer who builds AI tooling.
**Length:** 10 minutes, three beats, driven live by the operator.
**Everything below was rehearsed end to end on 2026-08-20 against a byte copy of
the live workspace.** Timings are measured wall time, not estimates.

The demo is terminal-only. Mission Control does not need to be running, and no
beat depends on it, on the network, on a phone, or on a model call. Nothing in
the demo merges, deploys, spends, or sends.

## Beat 1 — The portfolio holds the state (target 0:20 → 3:15)

### 1a. `arcadia portfolio` — 1.5s, 51 lines

**Appears:** four active projects with real open/in-progress/blocked/done
counts and a clarity breakdown, then "Waiting on you:" listing 16 open
Decisions, then `60 Actions in flight; 28 ready to work, 35 not yet.`

**Say while it renders** — read whichever numbers are actually on screen, they
drift as you work:
> "Four projects, sixty actions in flight. Twenty-eight are ready to work and
> thirty-five aren't — and it knows the difference, which is the only reason I
> can leave and come back."

**Then, pointing at the Decisions block — do not read them:**
> "And those sixteen are questions it needs *me* for. It stopped and asked
> instead of guessing."

### 1b. `arcadia docket` — 1.5s, 72 lines

**Appears:** Arcadia's own active plan, token impact, milestone, current Action
`make-test-action-state-aware`, its next action, five acceptance criteria, the
authorization line, then the standing constraints from `CONSTITUTION.md`.

**Say:**
> "This is the repo I'm standing in. One command, no model call, tells me the
> plan, the current action, and what 'done' means for it."

**Scroll to the bottom and land this — it is the line for her specifically:**
> "And that's the constitution, printed with every dispatch. The agent doesn't
> have to remember the rules; the rules ship with the objective."

### 1c. `arcadia docket --repo ~/Dev/PrivatePracticeNow/platform` — 1.8s, 66 lines

**Appears:** a completely different project, plan, and current Action, including
one very long next-action paragraph. **Do not read it.** Let it fly past.

**Say:**
> "Same command, different repository, different truthful answer. That paragraph
> is what a coding agent gets handed — I didn't write it this morning, it
> accumulated. All of that cost zero model tokens."

> **CUT LINE:** if the clock is past 3:00, skip 1c entirely. Beat 1 works
> without it and beat 2 is where the value is.

**Fallback:** all three commands read local SQLite and local git. There is no
network path to fail. If one errors, `arcadia portfolio` alone carries the beat.

## Beat 2 — Ideas come back on their own (target 3:15 → 6:30)

> This is the beat that carries the demo. If everything else runs long, protect
> this one.

### 2a. `arcadia back-burner list --fired yes` — 1.6s, 12 lines

**Appears:** exactly one item —

```
Fired conditions (1)
- bb_2f2e6f0b3fe94a35b6: ExecutionRequest (opportunistic)
  Input: Add a --brief flag to arcadia docket that prints the current Action without the constitution block, for screen sharing
  Project: Arcadia
  Surface: date 2026-08-21 — fired
```

**Say — slowly, this is the point of the whole demo:**
> "I had that idea yesterday, rehearsing this. It was a good idea and it was not
> what I was doing, so I gave it a condition — come back on the twenty-first —
> and I forgot it completely. Today is the twenty-first. It came back."

**Then:**
> "Most tooling has nowhere to put the good idea that isn't now. It becomes a
> TODO nobody reads, or it's gone. This is a third option: park it against a
> condition and actually forget it."

Type this in front of her:

**Appears:** `Execution path: Back Burner`, `Result: Captured in Back Burner.`,

**Say:**

## Beat 3 — It refuses to break things, and one last idea comes back (target 6:30 → 9:30)

### 3a. `arcadia tidy` — 4.5s, 15 lines

**Run this from `~/Dev/MR/Arcadia/arcadia`.** `tidy` defaults `--repo` to
wherever you are standing; from inside a worktree it reports on that worktree
instead and the beat loses its point.

**Appears, if PR #86 is NOT merged** — three sections:

```
Where the live work is:
  demo-rehearsal-2026-08-21 — …/.claude/worktrees/demo-rehearsal-2026-08-21
      1 commit on demo-rehearsal-2026-08-21 not on main; a remote copy exists.

Would retire (2):
  - worktree …build-demo-hero-vertical-slice-…
      Every commit on … is already on main.
  - worktree …fix-repo-path-resolution
      Every commit on … is already on main.

Nothing was changed. Re-run with --apply to retire the items listed above.
```

**Say:**
> "Three agent worktrees. It can tell finished work from live work — those two
> are merged and safe to retire, that one still has a commit that isn't on main,
> so it won't touch it. And it changed nothing. Everything destructive here
> makes you ask twice."

**Appears, if PR #86 IS merged:** no `Where the live work is` section, and
`Would retire (3)`. Then say instead:

> "Three finished agent sessions, and it tells me *why* each one is safe to
> retire — every commit is already on main. And it changed nothing. Everything
> destructive here makes you ask twice."

**Do not run `--apply`.** That is the point of the beat.

**Fallback:** `arcadia tidy` fetches from origin and takes 3.6s. If GitHub is
unreachable it may hang. If it does not paint within about five seconds,
Ctrl-C and run:

```bash
arcadia tidy --no-fetch --no-github
```

That version was timed at 1.3s, is fully offline, and prints the same four
sections with an honest caveat line about unverified merges. **Rehearse the
Ctrl-C.**

## What was deliberately left out

- **Mission Control (127.0.0.1:3020).** It works, and its Back Burner page shows
  a live `N fired` counter. It was cut because switching windows costs more
  seconds than it buys, and two of its cards render raw ingested text that looks
  broken on a projector. The terminal view of the same data is tighter and
  filterable.
- **The Project Detail demo hero and the PPN Candidate.** The Candidate is
  `http://127.0.0.1:4321`, Mac-only and not phone-reachable, and the
  candidate-to-release story needs more than three minutes to mean anything.
  It is the natural centrepiece of a *second*, longer demo.
- **`arcadia go`.** Its preview is genuinely safe and changes nothing, but it
  overlaps with `tidy` on the safety point and needs more setup narration than
  it repays in a ten-minute slot.
- **`arcadia mission-control fits --minutes N`.** Deterministic and a good line,
  but against this workspace it currently returns a single Life item, which is
  too thin to spend a beat on.
- **Promoting a fired item** (`arcadia back-burner promote <id>`). It works, and
  it is the honest "and then it becomes real work" ending — but it adds a fourth
  command to beat 3 and the fired list is already the emotional peak. Mention it
  in one sentence if she asks what happens next.
