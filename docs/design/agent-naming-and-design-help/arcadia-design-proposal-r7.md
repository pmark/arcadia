# Proposal: field-neutral design guidance (Phase 1, revision 7)

Revision 3 applied an adversarial domain-generality review (game, song, physical
space) and the operator's direction: website design first, then game design.
Revision 4 applied a simulated paper walk-through (one website persona, one game
persona); both passed with changes.
Revision 5 applies a second simulated round. Five operator personas: two
website, two game (one physical, one with two owners) and one song. The core
held in all five with no field-specific change, including the song. Only one
persona accepted the record unchanged. Most recurring defects were in how the
facilitator records and speaks. Two exposed gaps in the core itself: it assumed
a single decision owner, and near parks had no way back. Revision 5 adds Owner
and revival conditions for those. Evidence:
sim2-synthesis.md and the five sim2-*.md logs.
Revision 6 applies a critic's review of revision 5 (PR #1869 comment
6488956937). It makes record fidelity checkable each turn, separates an
owner's position from the evidence behind it, settles the two open gaps
(captured-only ideas; a hide-until wins over Visible for its duration), splits
the decision owner from whoever performs a move, and makes Milestone 1 a
two-session usefulness test against ordinary notes.
Revision 7 makes the persona an expert designer in whatever field is active,
by operator direction, and trims the record using a third simulated round: an
expert alone, an expert with a minimal record, and an expert with the full
revision 6 record, each over two sessions in website and game, scored by a
blind judge. The full record did best in both fields and the expert alone did
worst; without rules, the expert's opinions leaked into the operator's
positions. A next step on every open item proved harmful and is now limited to
items being worked on. Evidence: sim3/synthesis.md, sim3/judge.md and the
twelve sim3 session files.

## Outcome
The operator can brainstorm freely in any design field and trust three things:
- Every idea is kept verbatim, in its original medium, at the strength and
  scope the operator gave it.
- Every idea leaves the session settled, rejected on record, set aside with a
  condition for coming back, captured only, or open on the list; whatever is
  being worked on has one concrete next move with an owner.
- An expert designer in the field brings its craft and honest opinions, and
  those opinions stay recorded as the expert's, never as the operator's.

A design that is ready to commit reaches its owners as a design brief and as
governed Asks to settle.

## Milestones
1. **The field-neutral core is proven useful on paper.** The operator's own
   vague ideas, one website and one game, go through the core with no field
   guide and no code, across two sessions with a gap between them. Every idea
   ends settled, rejected, set aside with a condition, captured only, or open
   on the list, and whatever is being worked on has a next move. No step needs a
   field-specific assumption, and the operator would accept the record
   unchanged. Actually settling Asks stays the operator's choice.
   - The second session reopens a chosen element, reaches a parked item's
     revival condition, and brings back an absent co-owner with a different
     view (a persona plays one if the operator has none). It includes an
     interrupted session and a failed or inconclusive test.
   - Usefulness is compared with resuming the same design from ordinary notes.
     The measures are the number of corrections, time to recover context,
     ownership questions left unanswered, and whether the operator can name a
     useful next step without reading the whole record. The operator agrees
     the success criteria before the walk-through.
   - The results decide which core concepts earn their complexity before any
     field guide is written. Three simulated rounds with operator personas
     have been run (nineteen sessions across website, game and song); they
     found failure cases but cannot show reduced effort for a real operator.
2. **Website design field guide** (the first guide, by operator direction). One
   vague website idea goes through the core, enriched by a website field guide,
   to a ready-to-commit brief, with no website-only change to the core.
3. **Game design field guide** (the second guide, by operator direction). One of
   the operator's vague game ideas reaches a ready-to-commit brief the same way,
   with no game-only change to the core.

Later field guides follow the same path. None of them changes the core.

## Field-neutral core (plain language, not a schema)
- **Expert designer:** the persona who runs the conversation. It is an expert
  in whatever field is active: it brings that field's craft, frameworks, better
  tests and examples that worked or failed, and gives its honest opinion. Its
  expertise comes from the field's guide when one exists; with no guide it says
  it is working from general knowledge, recorded as agent inference. Its
  opinions are recorded as its own, attributed, and never placed among the
  owner's principles or positions. It argues a point when asked and does not
  repeat it once the owner has heard it. It never rewrites the owner's test
  bar, and it does not design or argue for its own ideas while an owner is
  away. It keeps the record as part of the conversation; record keeping is not
  a separate role.
- **Design brief:** one checked-in design Artifact per design. Its top is a
  short section an owner can read on its own: the experience target, the
  principles, the stated scope (each attributed to whoever said it), what is
  being worked on now with its next moves, and anything that was due and has
  not arrived.
- **Experience target:** the moment or result the whole design is judged
  against, in the owners' words. Examples: "on the twentieth night it finally
  turns and looks at you"; "the thing you feel when you put a heavy bag down".
  If none has been stated, one question asks for it. Probes test against it. A
  design may have more than one target, for example the lasting moment plus a
  festival-booth test.
- **Principles:** the owners' convictions and taste. Each is recorded at the
  strength it was given: a rule ("no passwords, ever") or a feel ("chord barely
  changes is the feel, not a law"). A principle is settled when stated, carries
  operator-practice provenance, and only its owner rewords it.
- **Learned:** what a probe or past attempt showed, judged against the bar the
  owner set (the expert may add its own reading, attributed, but never replaces
  the owner's bar), scoped to exactly what was
  tried and stating its limits ("tried on five commutes"; "that one steal rule
  felt mean"). Its result supports, contradicts or is inconclusive about the
  item it is linked to. A learned result is evidence, not a position and not a
  principle; a finished test never implies a successful result or a decision. It becomes a principle only when the owner says so. A probe's own
  acceptance bar may become a principle when the owner states it as one ("the
  real version has to be about as easy as a text").
- **Idea:** a verbatim capture of any size or medium. Never requires
  classification. A non-text idea (photo, voice memo, sketch, reference site) is
  kept as the original, attached or marked "to attach", alongside the owner's
  own description. Never transcribe it or normalize it to the expert's
  words. The original stays beside any interpretation the expert
  proposes (which elements it touches, how strong it is, what it rules out),
  and a correction stays visible rather than silently replacing the earlier
  reading. An idea can be marked "thinking out loud": captured only,
  with no position, owner or next move until the owner promotes it into the
  design.
- **Element:** a part of the design that matters, whether core, conditional or
  optional. Elements come from a field guide or emerge from ideas, and they
  inform each other (cycles are allowed). An idea may stay attached to several
  elements as one knot; separating them is only ever a suggestion. A link the
  expert infers is shown as a suggestion until the owner confirms it. An
  element may be recorded as one route to an experience target. This keeps
  cheaper substitutes open ("#2 is one way to get #5").
- **Owner:** who decides each element: one person, everyone jointly, or one
  person after a condition ("art, Dev after the two-day pass"). The default is
  the single operator. The decision owner is distinct from whoever performs an
  element's next move; neither role grants Arcadia execution authority. The
  views of an absent owner or stakeholder are kept attributed ("Ana says ...")
  and are never merged into the present owner's view. Agreement is recorded
  per owner against the version agreed to; a prediction ("Dev thinks Ana will
  accept") is not agreement.
- **Position:** the owner's stance, kept separate from the evidence linked to
  it ("leaning toward no-hand; tried on five commutes; still open"). Any
  position can be reopened, and positions move conversationally without
  ceremony. Options are recorded exactly
  as wide as stated: "hasn't seen a buy button that feels right" is not "won't
  have one".
  - open
  - candidates
  - leaning (with the owner's reason, when given)
  - chosen (an owner's Decision)
  - rejected (kept on record with its reason and no next move; the owner may
    still act against it)
- **Next move:** one per item being worked on now; other open items stay on
  the list without one until they are taken up. It names who does it.
  Moves the expert owns are marked as the expert's and never attributed to the
  operator. It names when only in the owner's terms: a date or a step the owner
  gave, such as "before I write a second verse"; the expert never invents a
  date. It lists only the items it actually
  settles. The kinds of move:
  - **ask:** one question.
  - **decide:** a Decision with options in the owner's words, each carrying its
    cost in the owner's numbers ("a two-day pass").
  - **probe:** the cheapest experiment that produces an Artifact. The owner says
    who performs it (chosen for a valid result), how it is judged ("gut"
    included), and how long it will really take. The material may set the pace
    ("the kiln doesn't care").
  - **waits on:** a named move that settles this item.
  - **park:** see below.
- **Park:** every park has a revival condition, near or far. The conditions are
  "revisit at the end of this design", a date ("the week after the festival"),
  a trigger ("if people ask 'can I keep that?'"), or held hidden until a date or
  step ("don't let it come back up before"). Ideas outside this design go to the
  Back Burner with a revival condition. An urgent real-world task outside the
  design goes to the operator's to-do (see Decision 4), not to the shelf.
- **Visible:** items the owner marks as energizing ("the bit I'm actually
  excited about") stay in view whatever their position or next move. An
  explicit hide-until overrides this only for its duration; the mark is kept
  and the item returns in view when the condition is met.
- **Advice:** the expert's opinions and warnings are given in conversation and,
  where they matter later, recorded as the expert's. Advice is never a gate or
  a next move, and never becomes the owner's position unless the owner says so.
- **Field guide (optional):** the expert's field knowledge: elements, competing
  frameworks, questions and probe types, each entry carrying provenance.
- **Ready to commit:** the core elements are chosen by their owners; for the
  experience target's riskiest assumption, the brief says what the learned
  results support, or records which remaining risk the owner knowingly
  accepts; every item still being worked on has a next move with an owner,
  and every set-aside item has a condition for coming back. "Not ready" is the normal end of a first
  session, and its output is the probes and the open Decisions.

## Behavioral brief
- **REQ-001 Capture first:** any idea, at any time and in any medium, is kept
  verbatim or as its original before any question is asked.
- **REQ-002 Next move:** every item being worked on leaves a session with
  exactly one next move, including "waits on". The move names its owner, its
  "when" only in the owner's terms, and only the items it settles. Other open
  items stay on the list without one. Settled items, principles, learned
  results, rejected items, set-aside items and captured-only ideas need none.
  Round 3 evidence: a next step on every open item gave one operator seven
  chores, of which she said she would do one.
- **REQ-003 Suggest, don't impose:** the expert proposes which elements an idea
  touches, which links connect them, and which element to work next (the
  operator's lead or the riskiest open one). Once an owner declines a
  suggestion, for example separating a knot, it is not asked again.
- **REQ-004 Related change:** changing a chosen element or a principle, or a
  learned result that a choice relies on, flags every element it informs and
  marks joint owners' agreement to the earlier version as missing. Already-settled governed work
  changes only through the existing amendment path.
- **REQ-005 No guide still works:** a field with no guide gets the generic core
  (experience target, audience, constraints, principles, riskiest assumption),
  and elements emerge from ideas.
- **REQ-006 Provenance:** every field-guide entry is marked as a cited source,
  operator practice or agent inference, reusing the `learn` provenance and
  confidence terms. Missing provenance fails validation. Where a field has
  competing frameworks, choosing one is a Decision. Third-party advice that an
  owner merely repeats ("someone on a podcast said ...") is an idea, not a
  guide entry.
- **REQ-007 Commit output:** a ready-to-commit design yields Ask previews only:
  the brief as an `artifact` Ask, plus Outcome, Milestone and Decision Asks,
  plus the probe list for Phase 2. Nothing self-approves, and the brief grants
  no authority.
- **REQ-008 View:** the owners can view the brief read-only. Any view is derived
  from the checked-in brief.
- **REQ-009 Owners' words:** options, probe judgments and estimates use the
  owners' vocabulary and numbers. The expert never asserts a probe's size.
  Core terms ("probe", "waits on", "position") stay internal. In conversation
  the expert says what the owner would say ("the test", "not decided"),
  and asks for a probe's who, how and how long conversationally, not as a
  checklist.
- **REQ-010 Record fidelity (new):** the record states exactly what was said, no
  more and no less. That covers strength (rule vs feel), scope (a learned result
  vs a principle), width (options are not narrowed) and direction (inferred
  links are suggestions). An owner who says "that isn't what I said" about any
  row is a failed record. At the end of each session, and whenever a change
  touches something chosen, the expert shows only the interpretations that
  changed, beside their originals, for confirmation; the owner never has to
  proofread the whole brief. (Round 3: an end-of-session read-back caught as
  many errors as checking every turn.) Regression cases: a narrowed
  option (Teo's "hasn't seen a buy button that feels right" recorded as "sell
  without a button") and a feel recorded as a rule (Sam's chord); from round 3,
  an inconclusive test recorded as "a clean pass" and "Maybe?" recorded as
  "skip it".
- **REQ-011 Owners (new):** each element has a decision owner. Absent owners'
  and stakeholders' views stay attributed. A joint decision is chosen only when
  every joint owner has agreed to the same version; the brief shows which
  agreement is still missing.
- **REQ-012 Revival (new):** every park, near or far, carries a revival
  condition. Hidden-until conditions keep an item out of view until they are
  met, including an item marked Visible.
- **REQ-013 Expert attribution (new):** every expert opinion that is recorded
  is attributed to the expert and kept apart from the owner's words; the
  owner's test bar and principles change only when the owner changes them.
- **REQ-014 Resumption (new):** a later session opens from the brief's top
  section, including anything that was due and has not arrived, before new
  ideas are taken.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Conflicting ideas stay as competing candidates until a Decision. An owner
    may dissolve a conflict by rewording their own principle.
  - A brainstorm resumes from the checked-in brief in a later session.
  - A brainstorm can start before any Project exists.
  - An owner may act against a rejected item.
  - Sensitive personal material is the owner's call alone, and the expert
    doesn't press it.
- **Non-goals:** generating the work; third-party guide sharing; AI deciding
  design quality (the expert gives opinions; the owners decide); an interactive
  board.

## Open Decisions (Phase 1)
1. **Architecture**
   - (A) A few built-in fields in code. Fastest, but every new field needs code
     changes.
   - (B) Field-neutral core only. Works for any field, but has no field
     knowledge.
   - (C*) Field-neutral core plus optional field guides as data. A new field
     needs only a guide, but this requires a guide format (new, analogous to
     Decision 0074's data-not-code principle). Round 2 supports this: three
     fields ran on the core alone.
   - (D) A code plugin system. Most flexible, but highest cost, and it drifts
     toward the excluded third-party sharing.
2. **Role for the expert designer** (a Behavior run between Phase 1 and Phase 2)
   - (A) A design Behavior followed by `planner`. No registry change or new
     authority, but no addressable expert: whoever plans that day follows it.
   - (B*) A named expert-designer profile on `planner` that carries that
     Behavior: one persona for every field, its depth coming from the active
     field guide. It is addressable by name and function and distinct from
     Ellis the Engineer, with no registry change or authority. Rounds 2 and 3
     found that voice, record fidelity and keeping expert opinion apart from
     the owner's are the hard part, which favours a dedicated profile with its
     own brief. The name is the operator's to choose (Iris was suggested).
   - (C) An eighth registered role. A first-class contract, but it changes the
     role registry and Decision 0114 delegation, and is an operator-only
     authority change.
3. **Field-guide authorship**
   - (A*) Operator-curated with agent research; the operator ratifies each
     guide. Trustworthy, but costs operator time per guide.
   - (B) Agent-drafted with a mandatory operator ratification pass. Faster, but
     risks confident shallow guidance if the review is light.
   - (C) Import external guides verbatim. Fastest, but carries copyright,
     staleness and fit risk.
4. **Probes and urgent to-dos the operator performs** (playtests, demo listens,
   mock-ups, the outside-design task that matters this week)
   - (A*) A Decision with an evidence Artifact attached for probes; urgent
     outside-design tasks go to the operator to-do list. No schema change, but
     probes do not appear in the work queue.
   - (B) An Action with a new operator responsibility. Visible and trackable,
     but it changes the dispatch and completion contract (responsibility today
     is autonomous | agent | requires_review | blocked, and completion needs a
     git candidate_revision), so it needs its own Decision.
   - (C) Operator to-do items only, for both. Cheapest, but the least governed.
5. **Revival condition for this proposal**
   - (A) A dependency on a named Action, which the operator picks, reaching
     done. It resurfaces automatically, but must name a real Action.
   - (B) Manual. It resurfaces when the operator browses the shelf. Truthful,
     but it may never resurface.

## Phase 2 open questions (not decided here)
- Where a brief lives before a Project exists, for example as the source-ref of
  an unscoped Back Burner item.
- The field-guide format and its home.
- Whether Decision-to-Decision links need enforcement. Out of scope until a
  brief proves it.
- The view's surface.
- Linking items captured in the existing /capture PWA to a brief. Deferred;
  mobile capture itself already exists.
- How owners who are not Arcadia operators (a co-founder, a bandmate) are named
  and attributed in a brief, given that Arcadia's governance has a single human
  operator. The views of non-operator owners are recorded; only the operator
  settles Asks.
- How near parks with date or trigger conditions are surfaced. Reuse Back
  Burner revival machinery scoped to the brief, or keep a brief-local list.
- How non-text originals (audio, images) are stored as brief attachments
  without leaking private material.

## Terminology
Use "field" and "field guide", not "domain" or "domain pack". Domain is a core
Arcadia term (a life or work area), and "domain pack" already means a business
vertical's copy rules. "Owner" here means a design's decision owner, not an
Arcadia operator or a Responsibility value.
